# Craft base (Arcano edition)

The principles of typography and page layout the MCP tool
`craft_reference` serves to an agent working on a drawing, so a decision
of craft rests on a reason it can give. Each section says where the
principle comes from: the reference books of the trade (Robert
Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*; Jan Tschichold, *The
Form of the Book*; Jost Hochuli, *Detail in Typography*; Hochuli and
Robin Kinross, *Designing Books*; Ellen Lupton, *Thinking with Type*),
and on top of them the house standard of Editora Arcano 3, which wins
where they differ. Portuguese words of the trade are in brackets.

## Justification

### Even spaces before even edges
Justified text is good when its word spaces look alike from line to
line; a straight right edge bought with gaps is worse than a ragged one
(Bringhurst). The checks measure each line's spaces against a
normal space of the same font and size: wider than 133% is loose
(linha frouxa), narrower than 80% is tight (linha apertada).

### What may move, and how far
Word spacing first, then letter spacing in small steps, glyph scaling
never in body text (Bringhurst; Hochuli). The house fixes
justification with the tracking of a whole paragraph, at most 5
thousandths of an em either way, so no line is visibly tighter than its
neighbours, and never so that a paragraph or a page ends elsewhere.

### Rivers
Gaps that line up over three or more lines make white rivers (rios) that
pull the eye down the page. They come from wide spaces in a narrow
measure; the fix is the same as for loose lines, or rewording, which is
the author's, never ours.

### Measure
45 to 75 characters a line for body text, 66 the ideal; in two columns
40 to 50 (Bringhurst). A narrow measure justified is the source of
most loose lines: rag it or widen it.

## Hyphenation

### Settings
Words of at least 5 letters, at least 2 letters before the hyphen and 2
after (3 after in Portuguese body text reads better), never more than
two hyphenated lines in a row (Bringhurst; the house says 3 at
most and prefers 2).

### Where not to break
Not the last word of a paragraph, not across the turn of a right-hand
page (the reader turns the leaf mid-word), not a proper name, not a
one-letter syllable, not a word already hyphenated by the author at
another point (Bringhurst). Headings are not hyphenated.

## Breaks

### Widows and orphans
An orphan (órfã) is a paragraph's first line alone at the foot of a page
or column; a widow (viúva) is its last line alone at the top of the
next. Both break the block of the page; the widow is the worse
(Bringhurst; Tschichold). Fix them by making a paragraph of the
page gain or lose a line with tracking within the limits, so the text
until the end of the chapter moves one line; never by changing the
leading of one page or by stretching spaces.

### A single word on the last line
A word alone on a paragraph's last line (palavra única) is a light
defect, never fixed at the cost of spacing: positive tracking up to the
house's 15 thousandths that brings one more word down, the paragraph
ending where it did. If nothing works within the limits, it is a
question for the author.

### Facing pages end together
The two pages of a spread end on the same line, except the page that
closes a chapter (Tschichold; Bringhurst). A spread one line
short on one side is fixed by moving a line, not by vertical
justification.

### Titles kept with the text
A heading never stays at the foot of a page without at least two lines
of its text after it (keep with next).

## Rag

### A ragged right edge
Flush-left text is set with even spaces; its right edge should move in
small steps, without wedges (lines growing or shrinking one after the
other), holes (one line much shorter between longer ones) or a regular
short-long alternation (Bringhurst; Hochuli).

### Breaking by sense
In titles, epigraphs and short texts set ragged, break where the
reading pauses: not after an article, a preposition or a conjunction
(o, a, de, em, que, e), not between a number and its unit, not inside a
name. Balance the lines of every text of more than one line: the house
always balances titles (equilibrar linhas).

### Poetry
The line breaks of verse are the author's; a turned-over line is
indented and set flush right or with a hanging indent, never re-broken.

## The grey of the page

### Even colour
A page read from a distance should be one even grey; lines lighter or
darker than the rest (open tracking, a different font, loose spacing)
show as stripes (Bringhurst; Hochuli). The density check compares
each full line with the median of lines of the same style and size;
more than 10% off is a light or dark line, a page more than 2.5% off
its neighbours is out of tone.

### Leading and the grid
Body text on a baseline grid, the leading of the body (usually 120 to
145% of the size); headings, quotations and notes take whole multiples
of the leading or are compensated so the text after them is back on
the grid (Bringhurst; Tschichold).

## Chapter openings

### On a right-hand page
Chapters open on a right-hand (odd) page at the house, with the same
drop (queda) for every chapter: the number and title at the same height,
the text starting on the same line of the grid (Tschichold).

### The first paragraph
No indent on the first paragraph of a chapter or after a heading; the
house opens with a drop cap (capitular) or small caps on the first words.

## Front matter and credits

### The house order
Half title (falsa folha de rosto), a blank page, title page (folha de
rosto), credits (página de créditos), then dedication, epigraph,
contents and the text. The title page is a right-hand page; the half
title and the title page show the same title.

### Credits
The credits page carries the ISBN, the catalogue record, the copyright
line and the house's fixed text of rights; a book without an ISBN on its
credits is not ready.

## Styles and importing text

### Everything through styles
Every paragraph has a paragraph style; the author's marks (italic, bold,
superscript, small caps) become character styles or stay as the
author's marks, never lost (Lupton; the house rule). Local formatting
over a style is a defect to clear, keeping the marks.

### Importing the author's file
After importing a .docx, compare the counts of italic, bold,
superscript and the paragraphs with the original: a silent loss of
marks is the worst error of composition because nothing fails.

## Contents, folios and running heads

### Folios
Page numbers (fólios) at the outer edge or centred at the foot, in the
size of the text or smaller, never on blank pages, chapter openings or
the front matter before the text (Tschichold). Planners and diaries
have no page numbers at the house.

### Running heads
The book's title on the left page, the chapter's on the right, in small
caps or italic, far enough from the text block to read as a frame.

## Micro-typography

### Quotes, dashes and ellipses
Curly quotes and apostrophes, never straight ones; the em dash (U+2014) for
dialogue and parenthesis in Portuguese, the en dash (U+2013) between numbers,
the hyphen only inside words; the ellipsis character (U+2026) instead of three
dots; one space after a full stop, none before punctuation (Bringhurst
5.2; Hochuli).

### Figures and capitals
Lining figures that stand aligned in tables and running text alike at
the house (never figures that dance); acronyms of four or more capitals
in small caps with a little letter spacing; thousands with a separator.

### Spaces
No double spaces; non-breaking spaces between a number and its unit,
after short abbreviations and before a dash that should not start a line.

## Checking and closing a book

### Before delivering
Text fully placed (nothing overflowing), no missing fonts, images at 300
ppi effective or more for print, everything inside the bleed or the
margins, the page count even (a multiple of the signature when the
printer asks), credits complete, a checkpoint saved before any fix in
bulk and a final look at every spread.

### Never change the author's text
Typography, spacing and breaks are ours; the words are the author's.
What cannot be solved without changing the text goes to the author as a
question.

## Interior design

### Proportions
The text block in proportion to the page; the inner margin the
narrowest, then the top, the outer and the bottom the widest
(Tschichold's canon); the house margins come from the house standard.

### Few fonts
One family for the text, at most a second for headings, chosen for the
book's reader and length; measure the font (font_metrics) before sizing
titles or fitting a box, never estimate.

## Vector drawing and generated images

### Drawing with real strokes
A drawing reads as drawn when its lines vary in width, start and end
thin, and overlap slightly where a hand would; few nodes on smooth
curves; closed shapes for ink areas rather than many thin strokes.
Edit vectors by their nodes, never rasterize them.

### Generated images
Use the newest model available; keep images linked and at their full
resolution, never upscaled by resampling in the layout.
